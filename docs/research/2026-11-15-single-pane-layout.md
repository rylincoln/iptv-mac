# Two columns instead of three: one sidebar, one main pane

Date: 2026-11-15
Status: research only. No source was changed, nothing was run in a window, and no stream was played.

## 1. The question

Can the window go from three columns (sidebar | channel list | player) to two: the sidebar as it is (search field, Recents, Favourites, Sports, All channels, groups), and one main pane that holds the video, the channel list, the search results and the playing channel's information, with overlays over the video where that suits? What layouts are feasible, what does Apple recommend, and what does each cost in this codebase?

The motive is the space under the video: beside a 320 pt list the video is small, and the details area under it is mostly empty.

## 2. The layout today

- `NavigationSplitView` with three columns: `SidebarView` (170/210/300 pt), `ChannelListView` (240/320/520 pt), and a detail column that holds only a clear view publishing its bounds as an anchor (`Sources/Tuner/Views/MainWindow.swift:27-36`).
- The player pane is not in the detail column. It is laid over the whole split view with `overlayPreferenceValue`, sized and offset to the detail column's anchor, or to the whole window when the video has it alone (`MainWindow.swift:41-50`). So the video's AppKit view never changes parent, and "video full screen" is only a different frame for the same overlay plus a request to `WindowBridge` to take the window full screen (`MainWindow.swift:98-103`, `Sources/Tuner/Views/WindowBridge.swift:76-120`).
- Inside the pane: the video at 16:9, fitted to the pane's width, then `ProgrammeDetails` under it when not full screen (`Sources/Tuner/Views/PlayerPaneView.swift:13-28`, `47-51`). Over the video: a click layer, the status overlays (Liquid Glass), the stats panel (plain 65% black, no blur: `Sources/Tuner/Views/StatsOverlay.swift:17`), and in full screen only, a Liquid Glass banner with channel, programme and times that shows while the pointer moves (`PlayerPaneView.swift:52-67`, `132-164`). The banner takes no clicks, and the pointer is tracked only in full screen (`PlayerPaneView.swift:53-59`).
- Focus is one `@FocusState` of `PaneFocus { channelList, player, search, covered }` (`MainWindow.swift:10`). `channelList` means whichever list shows in the middle column: the channels, or the search results while they cover them (`Sources/Tuner/Views/SearchResultsView.swift:67`). The channel list stays mounted under the results at opacity 0, not focusable, not hit-tested, and bound to `.covered` so that no focus request can land on it (`Sources/Tuner/Views/ChannelListView.swift:10-18`, `71-74`, `113`). It still has to swallow keys that arrive in the turn before focus moves on (`ChannelListView.swift:135-142`).
- The search field is a custom `TextField` in the sidebar's top safe-area inset, bound to `.search`, with its own handling for Down, Return and the insertion point after letters handed over from a list (`Sources/Tuner/Views/SidebarView.swift:20-24`, `48-77`).
- The window remembers the column visibility a search changed, so Escape can put it back (`columnsBeforeSearch`, `MainWindow.swift:20-24`, `52-85`, `121-136`). It tests for both `.detailOnly` and `.doubleColumn` as "sidebar hidden" (`MainWindow.swift:127`).
- A later programme chosen from the search results is shown in the details area in place of the playing channel's (`AppModel.chosenProgramme`, `Sources/Tuner/AppModel.swift:64-66`, `536-542`; `PlayerPaneView.swift:16-19`). Any design that removes the details area has to give this a new home.
- The design spec records the three-pane layout as **decided** (goal 1, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-iptv-mac-client-design.md:20`, and the drawing in 7.1). Adopting anything below means amending that.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Geometry: what a 16:9 video leaves over

This is arithmetic, not a source. Assumed: the default window of 1280 × 760 (`Sources/Tuner/TunerApp.swift:21`), ideal column widths, and about 52 pt for the title bar and toolbar **[not verified: assumed, not measured]**, so about 708 pt of height for content.

| Arrangement | Space for video | Video, fitted | Left over | Video's share of the window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today: sidebar 210, list 320 | 750 × 708 | 750 × 422 | 750 × 286 under it | 33% |
| List out of the way, sidebar kept | 1070 × 708 | 1070 × 602 | 1070 × 106 under it | 66% |
| List and sidebar out of the way | 1280 × 708 | 1259 × 708 | thin bars at the sides | 92% |
| List under the video at full pane width | 1070 × 708 | 1070 × 602 | 106 pt of list: two rows | 66%, list unusable |
| Minimum window 900 × 500, today | 370 × 448 | 370 × 208 | 370 × 240 | 17% |

Three things follow.

1. **The empty area is a consequence of the list being beside the video, not of the column count.** Any arrangement with a 320 pt list beside a 16:9 video in a landscape window leaves about 40% of the pane's height that the video cannot use. Moving the list from the split view's middle column into the main pane, and changing nothing else, gives the same picture as today.
2. **A list under the video does not fit** at the default size. It needs a window roughly 900 pt tall before five rows show.
3. So the real choice is between (i) keeping everything visible at once and using the leftover for information, as now, and (ii) **time-sharing the pane**: the video large most of the time, the list there when asked for. Time-sharing is what the three-column split view cannot do, and it is the actual gain from merging the columns (3.2).

### 3.2 Two-column `NavigationSplitView` on macOS

- Two columns are a first-class form: `init(sidebar:detail:)`, and `init(columnVisibility:sidebar:detail:)` to control it (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview).
- Apple documents the limit that was found here by rendering: "Some platforms don't respect every option. For example, macOS always displays the content column" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitviewvisibility). The middle column can never be hidden; a list that lives in the detail pane can be.
- Visibility values: `detailOnly` hides "all but the trailing column"; `doubleColumn` is defined only as hiding "the leading column of a three-column navigation split view" (same page). With two columns, a hidden sidebar should therefore read as `.detailOnly`. **[not verified]** which value the sidebar toggle writes into the binding with two columns on macOS 27.2; today's code already accepts both (`MainWindow.swift:127`), and should keep doing so until this is seen.
- Sizing: `navigationSplitViewColumnWidth(min:ideal:max:)` still sets the sidebar; "The navigation split view does its best to accommodate the preferences that you specify, but might make adjustments", and "Only some platforms enable resizing columns" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/navigationsplitviewcolumnwidth(min:ideal:max:)). The detail column takes the rest. A list placed inside the detail pane is no longer a split-view column, so **it loses its drag-to-resize divider** unless one is built (a nested `HSplitView`, or a fixed width). The HIG's Mac guidance for split views: "Set reasonable defaults for minimum and maximum pane sizes", "Consider letting people hide a pane when it makes sense", and "Provide multiple ways to reveal hidden panes. For example, you might provide a toolbar button or a menu command — including a keyboard shortcut" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views).
- The sidebar toggle in the toolbar is added by the split view and can be removed with `toolbar(removing: .sidebarToggle)` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/toolbar(removing:)). The HIG: "Consider letting people hide the sidebar... Avoid hiding the sidebar by default" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars).
- `NavigationSplitViewStyle` offers `balanced` (detail shrinks to make room for the leading columns) and `prominentDetail` ("attempts to maintain the size of the detail content when hiding or showing the leading columns") (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitviewstyle/balanced, `.../prominentdetail`). **[not verified]** whether `prominentDetail` makes the sidebar slide over the detail on macOS. It matters: `balanced` resizes the video when the sidebar shows; an overlaid sidebar would put system glass over live video.
- In the macOS 26 design the sidebar is "a pane of glass that floats above the window's content", and content "can appear over content from the adjacent split" only if the app opts in; inspectors "use an edge-to-edge glass that sits alongside the content" (WWDC25 "Build an AppKit app with the new design", 4:19-5:27, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/310/). The SwiftUI opt-in is `backgroundExtensionEffect()`, which duplicates the view into mirrored, blurred copies; its own documentation says "Apply this modifier with discretion... with consideration of visual clarity and performance" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/backgroundextensioneffect()). **Do not apply it to the video**: it is a second, blurred rendering of every frame **[inferred]**, and whether it can even capture an AppKit video view is **[not verified]**.
- **The anchor-overlay approach carries over unchanged in principle.** `overlayPreferenceValue` reads a preference from the view it is applied to and lays a second view over it (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/overlaypreferencevalue(_:alignment:_:)); nothing ties it to the column count. The detail pane can lay out anything (a list, a guide, an info strip) around a clear placeholder, and the placeholder's anchor says where the video goes. The video still never changes parent. **[inferred; not verified]** that the anchor resolves correctly from inside an `HStack`, a nested `HSplitView`, or beside an inspector in the detail column; today it is only proven for a placeholder that fills the column.
- One consequence of that approach to keep in mind: **the player overlay is above everything in the split view.** Anything meant to appear over the video (a drawer, a banner, controls) must live inside the overlay with the video, not in the detail column, which is underneath it.

### 3.3 Where the search field goes

- Apple's two Mac patterns: "Put a search field at the trailing side of the toolbar for many common uses... particularly apps with split views that need to search across multiple columns... Additionally, consider placing search in the toolbar if results appear in the detail view of your app", and "Include search at the top of the sidebar when filtering content or navigation there" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields). In the merged layout the results appear in the main pane, so **the HIG leans towards the trailing toolbar**, not the sidebar. The sidebar position the owner wants to keep is a documented pattern, but it is described for filtering the sidebar itself. This is a judgement for the owner, not a rule broken.
- API: `searchable` applied to the split view puts the field "on the trailing edge of the toolbar in macOS"; `placement: .sidebar` gives, on macOS, "a sticky header in the sidebar, attached to the toolbar" (inline with the sidebar's content only when built with Xcode 16 SDKs or earlier) (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/adding-a-search-interface-to-your-app, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/searchfieldplacement/sidebar). WWDC25 "Build a SwiftUI app with the new design", 11:32-12:18, says the same and that the modifier on the split view "indicates that search applies to the entire NavigationSplitView" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/).
- A system field can join the existing focus enum: `searchFocused(_:equals:)` binds a `FocusState` value to "the search field associated with the nearest searchable modifier" (macOS 15+, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/searchfocused(_:equals:)). Return is available through `onSubmit(of: .search)` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/managing-search-interface-activation).
- What the custom field does that no document promises for the system one: Down moves into the results (`SidebarView.swift:64-68`), and the insertion point is put after letters handed over from a list (`SidebarView.swift:69-74`). **[not verified]** whether either can be done on a `searchable` field. Given the history of focus bugs here, **replacing the custom field is a cost with no functional gain**; it is a separate decision from the column merge and can be left alone.

### 3.4 Options for sharing the main pane

**(a) List beside the video, inside the detail pane.** Plain stacks. Apple offers `ViewThatFits`, which "selects the first child whose ideal size on the constrained axes fits within the proposed size" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/viewthatfits), for choosing between arrangements by available space; the pane already uses it for the description (`PlayerPaneView.swift:181-184`). On its own this changes nothing visible (3.1). Its value is that the list becomes hideable, which the HIG encourages for panes (3.2). List under the video is ruled out by 3.1 at ordinary window sizes.

**(b) `inspector(isPresented:)` as a trailing, collapsible column.** Available on macOS 14+; "Trailing column inspectors have their presentation state restored by the framework" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/inspector(ispresented:content:)). Width by `inspectorColumnWidth(min:ideal:max:)`; inspectors "can collapse by default" but "aren't resizable by default", and a user-chosen width persists across launches (WWDC23 "Inspectors in SwiftUI", 4:50-5:14, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10161/). `InspectorCommands` adds a toggle on Control-Command-I (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/inspectorcommands), which does not collide with ⌘I for the stats (`Sources/Tuner/Commands.swift:67`). Inside a `NavigationSplitView` "the inspector should be placed in the detail column's view builder, or... entirely outside the navigation structure" (same session, 8:54-9:05). On macOS it never becomes a sheet (8:36-8:44). All of these are present in the installed macOS 27.2 SDK's SwiftUI interface (checked by searching it).
  - What an inspector is for: it "displays the details of the currently selected item, automatically updating its contents when the item changes" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/panels), and Apple's adoption guide says "Consider using split views to build sidebar layouts with an inspector panel" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass). That is an exact fit for **programme information** (description, up next, a later programme chosen from search).
  - For the **channel list** the fit is poor. A split view is defined as leading selections controlling "presentations in subsequent columns" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/navigationsplitview); a list on the trailing edge, driven by a sidebar on the far side of the video, reverses that. The one precedent Apple gives for non-detail content in an inspector is Shortcuts' action library, "content that supplements an app's main content" (WWDC23 session, 0:53-1:08). It would also only rebuild three columns with the third hideable.
  - An inspector sits alongside the content, so it costs video width, not compositing: with the list also showing, a 260 pt inspector leaves the video about 490 × 276 at the default window. It only makes sense with the list hidden, or instead of the under-video details.

**(c) List as a drawer over the video, on demand.** No system component does this; it is custom, built inside the player overlay. Apple's guidance bears on it in three places.
  - Material: "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer", and of a table specifically, "making it Liquid Glass would make it compete with other elements and muddy the hierarchy. So keep it in the content layer instead" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials; WWDC25 "Meet Liquid Glass", 13:04-13:24, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/). A channel list is content. So a drawer should have a standard material or an opaque or dimmed background, not glass.
  - Obscuring video: "In general, restrict this content so that it doesn't obscure media playback" (of extra information, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-video). Apple's tvOS section of the same page allows interactive overlays shown "gracefully", with "a clear way to dismiss the overlay"; that section is for tvOS, so it is an analogy here, not Mac guidance.
  - Full screen: "Continue to provide access to essential features and controls so people can complete their task without exiting full-screen mode" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/going-full-screen). Today ⌘F leaves full screen to search (`MainWindow.swift:121-126`). A drawer is the only option here that would let the owner browse or search **without leaving full screen**, which is its one real functional advantage.
  - An overlaid channel guide is the familiar television convention **[general knowledge]**.

**(d) Video as a small inset while browsing, large when watching.** The spec already asks for this for the Guide: the guide replaces list and player "at full width", and a playing channel "keeps playing in a small docked player fixed in the guide's bottom-right corner", explicitly not picture-in-picture (spec 7.4, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-iptv-mac-client-design.md:127`). With the anchor approach the docked player is just a small placeholder publishing the anchor; nothing about the video view changes **[inferred]**. The "watching" state, video filling the pane with the list underneath it, is what video full screen already does to the whole window (`MainWindow.swift:43-44`, `86-97`), restricted to the pane. The cost is in the interaction: if Return expands the video, zapping through channels (arrow, Return, arrow, Return) loses the list each time, which works against the spec's first aim, "navigate quickly" (spec section 1). The spec's own rule for the guide separates the two: choosing plays in the docked player; Return or double-click plays and returns to the large view (spec 7.4).

**(e) Others.**
  - *Video fills the window, windowed.* The overlay can already take the whole window; today that is tied to the window's full screen (`MainWindow.swift:98-103`). A windowed version of the same state needs no column change at all. It is the cheapest way to get a large picture and composes with every option above.
  - *A grid of channel tiles under the video* would use the 1070 × 106 strip or a taller window better than a one-column list, but `List`'s selection and arrow-key behaviour would have to be rebuilt by hand, as in the "focusable grid" recipe (WWDC23 "The SwiftUI cookbook for focus", 18:04-19:25, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10162/). Large, and it reopens keyboard handling from scratch. Not recommended.
  - *What Apple's own Mac media apps do* was not established from a source. AVKit's own Mac player offers controls "in a floating window over the video content", the style that "matches the look of QuickTime Player's playback controls" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avkit/avplayerviewcontrolsstyle/floating): transient controls over the picture, never a persistent list. Anything about the TV or Music apps' layouts would be **[general knowledge]** and is left out. The HIG has no page of its own for inspectors (the expected path returns 404); its inspector guidance is in Panels and Split views.

### 3.5 Where the playing channel's information goes

| Place | Apple guidance | Fits | Cost while playing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Under the video, adaptive** (full details when the list shows and 286 pt are free; one line when the video fills the pane and 106 pt are free) | `ViewThatFits` (3.4a). Nothing covers the video. | Everything there is today, including a chosen later programme | None: outside the video's area |
| **Window title and subtitle** (channel as title, programme and times as subtitle) | Toolbar leading edge carries "the view title"; on the Mac "window titles can display inline with controls" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars) | Name, programme, times. Not description or up next | None |
| **Banner over the video while the pointer moves** (exists in full screen) | "restrict this content so that it doesn't obscure media playback" (Playing video); regular glass is right where there is "a significant amount of text"; clear glass is for "components that float above media backgrounds" but needs dimming and bold content (Materials; "Meet Liquid Glass" 14:16-15:04; https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glass/clear). "Maintain one panel style when your app switches modes" (Panels) argues for the same banner windowed and full screen | Name, programme, times; a line of up next at most | Glass over live video while shown (3.6) |
| **Inspector** | The HIG's definition of one (3.4b) | Everything, including long descriptions and a chosen later programme | None in compositing; takes video width |
| **Popover** from a toolbar button or a results row | "Use a popover to expose a small amount of information"; "Consider using popovers when you want more room for content. Views like sidebars and panels take up a lot of space" (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers) | A chosen later programme, a description on demand | Transient |

Two notes. The title is free today in the sense that it only repeats the selected sidebar row (`MainWindow.swift:138-147`), but the subtitle carries the import message (`MainWindow.swift:105`), which would need to share or move. And the casting plan expects controls in the player (a cast button "in the player controls", device name, play/stop and volume in the pane while casting: spec 7.6), while full screen hides the toolbar (`MainWindow.swift:51`), so interactive controls over the video are coming regardless. The information banner and those controls should be designed as one overlay.

### 3.6 Materials over video: legibility and cost

- What glass does: it "blurs content behind it, reflects color and light of surrounding content, and reacts to touch and pointer interactions in real time" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views). Over live video the content behind changes every frame, so the effect is recomputed at the stream's frame rate, up to 59.94 per second on the owner's channels (`docs/spikes/2026-11-spike-results.md`) **[inferred]**.
- Apple on cost: "Creating too many Liquid Glass effect containers and applying too many effects to views outside of containers can degrade performance. Limit the use of Liquid Glass effects onscreen at the same time" (same page). Several glass shapes belong in one `GlassEffectContainer`, where "SwiftUI renders the effects together, improving rendering performance" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffectcontainer). And "avoid glass on glass" ("Meet Liquid Glass", 13:24-13:44).
- Apple on legibility: the regular variant "blurs and adjusts the luminosity of background content to maintain legibility" and suits text-heavy components; clear is for media backgrounds, with "a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity" if the content is bright (Materials). Appearance changes under Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast, and custom elements must be tested under them (Adopting Liquid Glass).
- What this means here **[inferred]**: a small transient banner (what exists) is within Apple's guidance and costs only while the pointer moves. A persistent glass panel, or a whole list on a blurred material, over the video is against both the HIG (content layer) and the owner's resource goal (spec goal 10). An opaque or plainly dimmed overlay, as the stats panel uses, is a simple blend with no blur. Anything beside or under the video costs nothing per frame.
- **[not verified]**, and worth measuring before any design leans on it: (1) whether Liquid Glass in SwiftUI actually samples the VLCKit view behind it or only SwiftUI content (how VLCKit's view renders was not examined); (2) the CPU and GPU cost of the existing banner over a 60 fps stream, shown against hidden. Both need the owner's streams and eyes. No measurement was made for this note.

### 3.7 Keyboard and focus

What Apple provides, and what it does not:

- `FocusState`: setting the value moves focus; and "binding the same value to two views is ambiguous... SwiftUI chooses the first candidate... also emits a runtime warning" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/focusstate). This is exactly why the covered channel list is rebound to `.covered` while the results list holds `.channelList`. Any design where two lists are mounted at once must keep that discipline.
- `focusable(_:interactions:)`: since macOS 14 custom views built with current SDKs "will always be focusable unless the client requests otherwise by specifying a restricted set of focus interactions" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/focusable(_:interactions:)). The covered list uses `focusable(!covered, interactions: [])` (`ChannelListView.swift:72`).
- `focusSection()` (macOS 13+) makes a region guide Tab order: SwiftUI visits "each of the modified view's focusable descendants before resuming the default sequence" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/focussection()). Useful if a drawer or an inspector adds focusable things; not used today.
- `defaultFocus(_:_:priority:)` (macOS 13+) is evaluated "when the window first appears, and when a focus state binding update moves focus automatically" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/defaultfocus(_:_:priority:)). It could replace the `onAppear` that gives the list focus (`ChannelListView.swift:114-116`), but changing that is not required by any option.
- Nothing in these documents covers the hard cases this codebase has met: a mounted but hidden list, keys queued before focus moves, or focus across the boundary between a split-view column and a view overlaid on it. Those remain trial in a running window.

Per option, exactly what would change:

- **(a), list in the pane, hideable.** `PaneFocus` is unchanged. The list keeps `.channelList` and the results keep covering it. New: one more reason for the list to be out of use (hidden), which should reuse the covered treatment and `.covered` binding rather than add a case; a hand-over when it hides while focused (to `.player`) and when it shows (to `.channelList`); the return from full screen, which sets `.channelList` unconditionally (`MainWindow.swift:94`), must go to `.player` when the list is hidden; and a search begun while the list is hidden must show it, and Escape put it back. That last is a second copy of the `columnsBeforeSearch` logic, the part of `MainWindow` with the most special cases (`MainWindow.swift:20-24`, `52-85`). The risk is managed by making it one remembered value for "what the search revealed" (sidebar and list together), not two.
- **(b), inspector for information only.** No focus changes if its content is text; a scrolling description adds one Tab stop **[inferred]**.
- **(c), drawer.** Escape gains another meaning ahead of the existing chain (`MainWindow.swift:52-67`); the list is hidden for a second reason (drawer closed) on top of covered; pointer tracking, now full screen only (`PlayerPaneView.swift:53-59`), must run windowed; the drawer and the player share the overlay, so clicks on the video must close the drawer without toggling full screen. The most new focus routing of any option.
- **(d), inset and large.** One more two-state hand-over shaped like the full-screen one (`MainWindow.swift:86-97`): large gives `.player`, small gives `.channelList`. The list under a pane-filling video is in the position it is already in under a window-filling one.
- **Search field as `searchable`.** `.search` would bind through `searchFocused(_:equals:)`; Down-into-results and the insertion-point rule have no documented equivalent (3.3).

### 3.8 What does not change, whichever option

The model is independent of the layout: the minute refresh runs on window visibility, not on which views show (`AppModel.swift:431-475`); selection following across reloads and favourites is all in the model (`AppModel.swift:379-414`, `710-735`); search scheduling likewise. One small thing to decide: a hidden list would still redraw its visible rows' progress bars each minute, as it does today under a full-screen video. It is cheap; the Sports section already skips its reload while covered (`AppModel.swift:470-474`), and a hidden list could follow the same rule.

## 4. Options

Sizes: small is a day or less in one or two files; medium is several files and a by-eye pass; large is new interaction design plus focus work.

## 5. Recommendation

**Do A, with option 0 alongside it, and keep the search field where it is.** In steps, each checkable on its own:

1. **Restructure only.** Switch to the two-column split view and put the channel list and the video's placeholder side by side in the detail pane. Nothing should look or behave differently. This step exists to prove, by eye and before anything depends on it, the three unverified points: the anchor from inside a stack, the visibility value with two columns, and the Tab order.
2. **Make the list hideable** with one menu command, shortcut and toolbar button, treating hidden exactly like covered. Fold "sidebar revealed by search" and "list revealed by search" into one remembered value.
3. **Make the details adapt** to the space under the video: full details when there is room, one line (channel, programme, times, next) when the video fills the pane. No overlay is needed for information at all in a window, so nothing is composited over the picture while watching.
4. **Add the windowed "video fills the window" state** (option 0) so the largest picture is one key away without full screen, with the existing banner.

Reasons:

- It delivers what the measurements say is available: the video goes from a third of the window to two thirds (list hidden) or nine tenths (option 0), on demand. No arrangement can do better while a 320 pt list is on screen (3.1).
- It is the option that changes focus routing least. `PaneFocus` keeps its four cases, the covered mechanism is reused and not multiplied, and the model is untouched (3.7, 3.8).
- It adds no per-frame cost. The list sits beside the video, the information under it. Glass over video stays what it is now: a small banner while the pointer moves (3.6). This matches both the HIG's line that Liquid Glass is not for content and the owner's resource goal.
- It matches documented Mac guidance rather than working around it: a hideable pane with a toolbar button and a menu command, in a split view whose un-hideable middle column Apple itself documents.
- It prepares the later plans. The Guide's "replace list and player with the guide, dock the video small" becomes "the main pane shows the guide, and the placeholder is small", which is option D's mechanism arriving when the Guide needs it. A Sources screen can be another main-pane view with the same docked video. Casting's controls belong to the one overlay over the video, with the banner (3.5).

What is deliberately not recommended now: the drawer (C) unless browsing inside full screen becomes something the owner wants enough to pay for, since it is the largest and riskiest piece and the only one that composites a list over live video; the inspector for the list (B2); the system search field (E). The inspector for information (B1) is a sound, cheap addition if step 3's one-line strip proves too little, and nothing in A prevents adding it later.

## 6. Open questions for the owner

1. **Time-sharing or everything at once?** The arithmetic says a large video and a visible list cannot both be had in an ordinary window. Is "list hidden most of the time while watching, one key to bring it back" the experience wanted?
2. **With the list hidden, what do the arrow keys do?** Nothing (as in full screen now), or channel up and down through the hidden list? The second is new behaviour and belongs in a spec, not in this change.
3. **Should a search started with the list hidden put it back on Escape**, as the sidebar is put back today, or leave it showing?
4. **Is the list's drag-to-resize divider needed?** Keeping it means a nested split view inside the pane (one more thing to verify with the anchor); dropping it means a fixed width.
5. **Search field: sidebar (as now) or trailing toolbar (the HIG's lean for this layout)?** Recommended: leave it.
6. **Information: is one line under a pane-filling video enough**, with the banner in full screen, or is a description wanted on screen while watching (then B1)?
7. **Window title:** keep the section name, or show the playing channel and programme there? If the latter, where does the import message go?
8. **The spec records three panes as decided** (goal 1, section 7.1) and describes the Guide as returning "to the three-pane view" (7.4). If A is chosen those passages need rewording before plan 2 is written.

## 7. Not verified

- Which `NavigationSplitViewVisibility` value a two-column split view reports on macOS 27.2 when the sidebar is hidden from the toolbar.
- That the detail-column anchor resolves correctly from a placeholder inside an `HStack`, inside a nested `HSplitView`, or beside an inspector; and that an inspector lies under the player overlay when the video takes the whole window.
- How a mounted `List` of thousands of rows behaves when given no width or moved aside, as opposed to opacity 0 at full size (the only hidden form proven here).
- Tab order between the sidebar, a list in the detail pane, and the overlaid player after the restructure.
- Whether `prominentDetail` overlays the sidebar on the detail pane on macOS.
- Whether Liquid Glass samples the VLCKit video behind it, and what the banner costs over a 60 fps stream. No profiling was done; no stream was played.
- The height of the title bar and toolbar (52 pt assumed in 3.1).
- Whether a `searchable` field allows a Down-arrow handler and control of the insertion point.
- How Apple's own Mac media apps arrange list and video: not established from any source, and left out of the reasoning.
